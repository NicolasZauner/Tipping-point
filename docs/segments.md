# Adopter groups (TP P4)

The model splits each market's drivers into five groups, each with its own switching threshold on
the TCO ratio (`docs/model-spec.md`). This file fixes how big each group is, where the sizes come
from, and how sure we are. Generated by `npm run data:segments`; the numbers below are copied
from `src/data/generated/groups.json`.

## Sources

The survey is the EAFO Consumer Monitor 2023 (October 2023), run by VUB-MOBI for the European
Commission's DG MOVE in twelve EU countries, all six of our markets among them. We use the
descriptive-statistics pages Nico downloaded, one per country plus the EU total. They hold
counts per answer, not respondent records, which is enough because every group is a sum of
answer counts. The parser recomputes every printed proportion from its count and fails on any
mismatch, so a misread column can't slip through. Germany cross-checks against its country
report (37% within 0 to 5 years, 29% "no").

Innovators come from the ACEA report "Vehicles on European roads" (January 2025 edition): the
BEV share of cars in use in 2023, the year of the survey. The 2024 shares from the 2026 edition
are kept next to them in `src/data/sources/acea-fleet.json` for the trend.

Reuse: the descriptive-statistics pages are Zenodo record 14523769 (VUB, December 2024) under
CC BY-SA 4.0, so the derived group sizes carry the same licence; the country reports are
Commission material under CC BY 4.0. The app credits "EAFO Consumer Monitor 2023, VUB-MOBI for
the European Commission (DG MOVE), CC BY-SA 4.0" and "ACEA" on the sources page.

## The question and the mapping (A8)

The survey asks non-BEV drivers whether they would consider buying a BEV and in what time frame.
Seven answers: within 0 to 2, 2 to 3, 3 to 5 or 5 to 10 years; yes but without a time frame;
I don't know; no. The wording is the same in all six country pages (the parser requires the
exact seven answers and fails otherwise).

Claude drafted the mapping from the question wording and the answer options; Nico signs it off.
The default reads the answers as a ladder of commitment:

| Group          | Answers                                  | Why                                                                                           |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Innovators     | already drive a BEV (fleet share)        | They switched before cost parity; the survey's BEV drivers were oversampled, the fleet wasn't |
| Early adopters | within 0 to 2 or 2 to 3 years            | A concrete near-term plan: they move at the first ratio near parity                           |
| Early majority | within 3 to 5 or 5 to 10 years           | Willing, but waiting for prices, range or chargers to improve                                 |
| Late majority  | yes but no time frame, or "I don't know" | Open in principle, no plan; they follow once BEVs are normal around them                      |
| Laggards       | no                                       | Rejects the idea today; needs a ratio well below 1, or never switches by 2035                 |

The alternative, the plan's first draft ("five-year cut"), counts everyone within five years as
an early adopter, puts 5 to 10 years and "no time frame" in the early majority, and leaves
"I don't know" alone in the late majority. It runs as a sensitivity.

Why the ladder is the default: "within 0 to 5 years" is 37% of German non-BEV drivers, far above
the 14% who name a BEV as their next car (table `drivetrain-next-car`). Calling all of them early
adopters would make the early group the largest in every southern market, which no adoption
history supports. The ladder keeps the early group to people with a plan inside one typical
holding period. "I don't know" sits with the undecided late majority rather than the laggards,
because it isn't a refusal.

## How the numbers are made

Innovators are the 2023 BEV fleet share, with a range of 0.8 to 1.25 times it: some fleet BEVs
are company cars rather than early private adopters, and the fleet grew between survey and
forecast start. Each other group is (1 − innovators) × its share of the non-BEV answers.

The interval is a 95% Wilson interval with a design effect of 1.5, so 1,698 German answers count
as about 1,130. The panel is an unweighted online sample, which is less informative than a random
sample of the same size; 1.5 is a common allowance for such panels and widens each interval by
about a fifth. Monte Carlo draws group sizes from a Dirichlet with the same effective size, so the
draws always add to 1.

Code: `scripts/lib/eafo.ts` (parser), `src/model/segments.ts` (mapping, Wilson, sizes, pure and
tested), `scripts/build-segments.ts` (writes the JSON with a passport per value).

## What the sizes don't say, and what absorbs it

Spain and Italy have the largest early groups and the smallest laggard groups, yet the lowest
BEV fleet shares (0.6% and 0.5%). That is the say-do gap: intentions there are high, purchases
are held back by price, chargers and grants. The model doesn't fix this in the group sizes. Each
market gets a fitted threshold shift (γ in the model spec) that moves all its groups' thresholds
together, and the calibration against 2018 to 2025 registrations sets it. A large γ for Spain is
the expected result, not a fault.

The survey asks licensed drivers, not new-car buyers. New-car buyers are older and richer than
drivers on average, and company cars are a third or more of registrations in Germany and Belgium.
The group sizes apply to private buyers; company buyers get their own thresholds in the model
(buyer type "company"), fitted rather than taken from the survey.

Belgium has the smallest sample (1,321 answers) and the widest intervals. The Netherlands has the
largest "no time frame / don't know" group (36%), which is plausible for a market where BEVs are
already common and the decision feels less urgent; it could also mean the Dutch panel read the
question differently.

## Is 2023 still current? The 2025 wave as a cross-check

The 2025 wave (fieldwork October to November 2025, 3,019 drivers across the EU-27) asks the same
question but has 79 to 563 answers per market, too few to rebuild the groups. Its Figure 21 shows
four coarser buckets for five of our markets (no Belgium): near term (0 to 3 years), later or
unsure (3 to 10 years plus "yes, no time frame"), no, and don't know. That last bucket also holds
the BEV drivers and people with no plan to buy a car, who weren't asked (10.5% EU-wide), so it
runs high. Regrouping the 2023 counts the same way:

| Market | 2023 near / later / no / don't know | 2025 near / later / no / don't know | 2025 answers |
| ------ | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------ |
| DE     | 23 / 31 / 29 / 17                   | 22 / 31 / 21 / 26                   | 563          |
| FR     | 23 / 23 / 29 / 24                   | 18 / 20 / 33 / 29                   | 444          |
| IT     | 28 / 36 / 17 / 19                   | 31 / 33 / 19 / 18                   | 394          |
| ES     | 29 / 41 / 14 / 16                   | 24 / 35 / 23 / 18                   | 320          |
| NL     | 15 / 37 / 29 / 19                   | 18 / 29 / 17 / 37                   | 124          |

The near-term group, which becomes the early adopters, moved by five points or less everywhere.
France and Spain turned more negative. Nico accepted the 2023 basis on 21 Oct 2026 on condition
that the app says so: every group size carries a "2023 survey" badge, and the Segments screen
shows this table. The 2025 EU totals were checked against VUB's question-by-question file
(No 26.2%, don't know 15.9%, no time frame 14.9% of those asked; next car BEV 13.9%).

Sources for the 2025 wave: the report "Consumer monitor 2025: EU drivers' view on electric cars"
(European Commission, March 2026; Figure 21 for the market buckets) and the dataset "European
Alternative Fuel Observatory Consumer Monitor 2025 (EAFO 2025)", Moonen, Van den Steen,
Vanhaverbeke and Verbist, Zenodo, September 2026, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22939459,
CC BY-SA 4.0. The dataset holds EU totals only, no market breakdown.
